# Findings

**Headline question:** Is Maxcha profitable, and what should I change to earn more?

**Short answer:** Yes. Over 9 weeks (50 cups) Maxcha made ₱7,062 in revenue and ₱1,947.50 in net profit, a 27.6% net margin. Break-even was about 26 cups.

---

## Q1 – Which drinks make the most profit?
**Finding:** Matcha Iced 12 oz (₱1,234.26 profit, 60.9% margin) and Hojicha Iced 12 oz (₱1,066.71) together make about 57% of gross profit. Half of the menu (5 of 10 drinks) sold only 1–2 cups.

**So I should:** keep Matcha and Hojicha 12 oz front and center and never run out of their ingredients. I should promote the lemonade as a demand test, and treat the 1–2-cup drinks as a guess, not a result.

## Q2 – Is 16 oz worth pushing over 12 oz? (at today's menu prices)
**Finding:** A 16 oz cup earns ₱14–21 more profit than a 12 oz cup, with a margin only 1–2 points lower. But 72% of cups sold are 12 oz. White Chocolate Matcha 16 oz is the best, at ₱107.30 profit per cup.

**So I should:** push the 16 oz upgrade, since each upgrade adds profit without lowering the margin much.

## Q3 – Do add-ons help?
**Finding:** Cups without an add-on: 36 cups, ₱77.51 profit per cup, 57.46% margin. Cups with an add-on: 14 cups, ₱87.22 profit per cup, 55.35% margin.

**So I should:** offer add-ons more often, since they earn about ₱9.71 more per cup even at a slightly lower margin.

## Q4 – How many cups do I need to break even?
**Finding:** Average profit is ₱80.23 per cup and operating expenses are ₱2,064, so break-even is about 25.7 cups. I sold 50, which is 24.3 cups above break-even.

**So I should:** use about 26 cups as my target for covering costs, and treat every cup after that as profit.

## Q5 – What drives the cost of my best seller (Matcha Iced 12 oz)?
**Finding:** Cost per cup is ₱53.70 (including 5% wastage). Matcha powder is ₱28.64 (56% of ingredient cost), whole milk ₱13.18 (26%), and packaging about ₱5.36 (10%).

**So I should:** compare matcha powder suppliers and bulk prices first, because it is more than half of my cost.

## Q6 – How does demand change week to week?
**Finding:** Cups per week: 11, 14, 2, 9, 1, 5, 1, 5, 2 (7 Sep to 2 Nov). The first four weeks sold 36 of 50 cups. The low weeks match weeks I was busy with my thesis, and the 26 Oct spike was one order.

**So I should:** keep a steady selling routine (regular posts and promos), since demand looks tied to how much I promote and when I'm available.

## Q7 – Is Maxcha really profitable after all expenses?
**Finding:** Revenue ₱7,062, gross profit ₱4,011.50, operating expenses ₱2,064, net profit ₱1,947.50, net margin 27.6%. Equipment is 71% of operating expenses; without it the margin would be about 48%. Ingredient and packaging restocks (₱6,504) are excluded from expenses because they are already counted in COGS.

**So I should:** not panic about the 27.6% margin, since equipment is mostly one-time. I should track margin again once equipment spending stops.
